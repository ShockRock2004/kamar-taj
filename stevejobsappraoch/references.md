# Evidence and sources for the Steve Jobs approach

Use only these quotes and facts when you attribute something to Jobs. They are paraphrased or quoted from the sources at the bottom.

## Keynote production (how the "Stevenote" was actually made)

- **Weeks of preparation.** Jobs reviewed every candidate product and technology weeks ahead and decided whether each was "keynote-ready", often from unfinished software. (Mike Evangelist, ex-Apple, via allaboutstevejobs.com)
- **"Rules of the Road."** Staff got watermarked booklets that listed every milestone with a named DRI. Matt Drance: "72 sleepless hours for something that Steve Jobs showed on stage for 9 seconds."
- **Two days of rehearsal.** On day 1 he worked the weakest segments with product and engineering managers in the room as his test audience, asking for their feedback. Day 2 had one or two full dress rehearsals. Outside presenters joined only on day 2.
- **Guest speakers were drilled.** A partner executive spent a week and a half in Cupertino, presenting to more and more senior executives and finally to Jobs. Guests were told, not asked, what to wear and say and when to rehearse.
- **Demo engineering.** The iPhone shown at Macworld 2007 was a custom build that took hundreds of engineering hours just to drive the projector. Before Macworld 2005 a film crew was flown to Hawaii to shoot a wedding for the iMovie demo.
- **Running order.** Metrics first (revenue, stores), then segments (software updates, guests, demos, ads), then the big reveal after "One more thing…", sometimes with a musical close.
- **Failure handling.** When his clicker failed at Macworld 2007 he stayed calm and told a story while it was fixed.
- Evangelist on the result: "an incredibly complex and sophisticated blend of sales pitch, product demonstration and corporate cheerleading, with a dash of religious revival."
- The "reality distortion field" (Bud Tribble's phrase) was charisma *plus* meticulous, calculated production.

## Carmine Gallo's framework (*The Presentation Secrets of Steve Jobs*, 2009)

The book has 3 acts and 18 scenes: **Create the Story** (scenes 1–7), **Deliver the Experience** (8–13), **Refine and Rehearse** (14–18). It includes "Intermission: Obey the Ten-Minute Rule" and "Scene 11: Share the Stage". The ten techniques from Gallo's summary:
1. **Plan in analog.** Storyboard with pen, paper and whiteboard before opening any software. Demos, videos and guests are collected before the slides are made.
2. **A Twitter-friendly headline (140 characters or less).** MacBook Air: "The world's thinnest notebook."
3. **Introduce the antagonist.** IBM ("Big Blue") in 1984: "IBM wants it all." For the 2007 iPhone the villain was the problems with existing phones.
4. **Focus on benefits.** iPhone 3G: "twice as fast at half the price." Time Capsule: your irreplaceable photos are protected automatically.
5. **Rule of three.** In September 2009: iPhones, iTunes, iPods, with verbal signposts ("the first thing… now the second").
6. **Sell dreams, not products.** iPod, 2001: "In our own small way we're going to make the world a better place." In 1997: "in that craziness we see genius."
7. **Visual slides.** No bullets, and it's hard to find seven words on ten of his slides, against about 40 words on an average slide. MacBook Air coming out of a manila inter-office envelope. "Picture superiority."
8. **Make numbers meaningful.** Schiller in 2009: 220M iPods = 73% market share, versus Microsoft "pulling up the rear" at 1%.
9. **Zippy words.** The iPhone 3G was "amazingly zippy". No "best of breed" or "synergy".
10. **A "holy smokes" moment.** The emotionally charged event of Macworld 2007: "an iPod, a phone, an Internet communicator… are you getting it? These are not three devices. This is one device!"
11. **Practice a lot.** Gallo: Jobs got visibly better from 1984 to 1997 to 2007, and the effortlessness came from hours of practice.

## Product and focus philosophy

- **"Innovation is saying no to 1,000 things."** "Focus… means saying no to the hundred other good ideas that there are. You have to pick carefully."
- In 2003, about iTunes: "We don't want a thousand features. That would be ugly."
- **Top 100 retreat.** On the last day he asked "What are the 10 things we should be doing next?", cut the bottom seven, and said "We can only do three." People were chosen as the ones you'd take on a lifeboat to your next company.
- **The 1997 2x2.** About 40 products became 4: Consumer/Pro × Desktop/Portable.
- **"Start with the customer experience and work backwards to the technology."** (WWDC 1997) "What incredible benefits can we give to the customer? Where can we take the customer?"
- **"Simplicity is the ultimate sophistication."** "Design is not just what it looks like and feels like. Design is how it works." (NYT, 2003)
- **iPod.** No on/off switch, no battery door, no screws. The scroll wheel came from his refusal to add buttons. Any song in three clicks or fewer.
- **Isaacson's 14 lessons** (HBR, April 2012): focus; simplify; take responsibility end to end; when behind, leapfrog; put products before profits; don't be a slave to focus groups; bend reality; impute; push for perfection; tolerate only A players; engage face-to-face; know both the big picture and the details; combine the humanities with the sciences; stay hungry, stay foolish.
- **Back of the fence.** His father taught him to finish the unseen side, so the Mac's circuit board had to look good too. (Isaacson)
- **"People don't know what they want until you show it to them."** (Isaacson)
- **Leapfrog.** Apple missed CD burning, and answered with iTunes, the iPod and the iTunes Store.
- **Weekly reviews.** On Mondays the executive team reviewed the top 4–6 projects in detail. Every item had one DRI, never two.
- **Creative selection** (Ken Kocienda). Small, frequent demos to Jobs and Forstall, with decisions made in the room. Teams "lived on" the product (dogfooding). Seven elements: inspiration, collaboration, craft, diligence, decisiveness, taste, empathy.
